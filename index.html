03 / A BRIEF HISTORY OF MONEY

From shells to 数字.

Money has changed shape many times. Its purpose has remained remarkably consistent: helping people exchange value.

5,000+ years

There was no single moment when money was invented. Different societies developed different ways to record debts, store value and make trade easier. Here are some major milestones in that long story.

01
c. 3000–2000 BCE

Before coins: barter, goods and records

Communities exchanged goods directly and used valuable commodities such as grain, livestock, shells and metal. In Mesopotamia, clay tablets recorded debts and quantities of goods, helping trade operate beyond immediate swaps.

BarterCommodity moneyAccounting
𓆙 CLAY TABLET
02
c. 7th century BCE

The first widely recognised coins

Electrum coins, made from a natural mixture of gold and silver, appeared in Lydia in western Anatolia. Standardised weight and official marks made coins easier to recognise and use in trade.

ElectrumLydiaStandardisation
◉EARLY COINAGE
03
c. 7th–11th centuries CE

Paper money takes shape in China

Merchant credit instruments developed into government-issued paper currency during the Song dynasty. Paper notes were lighter to carry than strings of metal coins and helped support long-distance commerce.

ChinaJiaoziPaper notes
紙PAPER MONEY
04
Middle Ages – 19th century

Banks, bills and global trade

Bills of exchange, deposit banking and banknotes expanded across trading networks. Governments and banks experimented with systems linking paper money to precious metals, while international trade made exchange rates increasingly important.

BankingBanknotesGold standard
▤BANKING ERA
05
1944–1970s

Bretton Woods and floating exchange rates

The Bretton Woods system established fixed exchange-rate arrangements centred on the US dollar, which was convertible into gold for official foreign holders. The system broke down in the early 1970s, and major currencies moved towards floating rates.

Bretton WoodsUS dollarFloating rates
◎GLOBAL SYSTEM
06
1990s – 2002

Digital finance and the euro

Electronic payments and online banking transformed how people accessed money. The euro was introduced as an accounting currency in 1999, with euro banknotes and coins entering circulation in 2002.

Electronic paymentsEuroOnline banking
€EURO ERA
07
2009 – today

Mobile money, crypto and CBDCs

Smartphones made instant digital payments part of everyday life. Bitcoin introduced a decentralised cryptocurrency in 2009. Central banks are also researching or piloting central bank digital currencies (CBDCs), while cash remains essential in many communities.

Mobile walletsCryptoCBDCs
₿DIGITAL VALUE

This is a simplified global overview, not a complete history of every monetary system. Money developed differently across regions and cultures.
